Where the numbers on this display come from, and how much to trust each one.

Data source

Replay of three Oslo school buildings, hourly, from 24 Sep 2022 to 31 Mar 2023 (SINTEF COFACTOR-SBHUB, Lien et al. 2025, CC BY 4.0). Times are a fixed UTC+1 all year.

Heat use, outdoor temperature, sun and wind are measured values of those buildings. The dataset has no indoor temperature, occupancy or equipment data, so none is shown for the buildings. The clock is shared: the playback console decides what hour the replay is at, and this page never shows anything later.

Smart heating: a simulated classroom

Indoor temperature and heating power on the Smart heating page come from a digital twin of one 60 m² classroom, run on the weather of the selected building for the last completed days before the clock. It is a simulation, not a measurement.

Current heating is how the school is heated today: fixed radiator timers with a thermostat, ventilation 07:00–16:00 that is boosted by 50 % when the room reaches 24 °C, and a ventilation coil that always heats the air to 21 °C. Smart heating uses the weather forecast: it starts the radiators at the right time, looks ahead with the thermostat, and sets the ventilation coil target that lower when the room is warm. It is given a perfect weather forecast and a perfect room model, so it shows the best case.

Heat-usage forecast

An AI model (LightGBM and GRU) predicts the whole building's heat use 1, 6 and 24 hours ahead from the last week of readings. It was trained on data before 1 March 2022 and is applied to the replayed window.

The weather of the coming hours is the measured weather replayed as a perfect forecast, so real forecast errors are not included and the accuracy is an upper bound.

Room model

The twin's room has two heat stores: a fast air node (about 11 minutes) and a slow wall node (about 91 hours). The slow one is the thermal inertia that lets a room overshoot after the radiator is closed.

Cair · dTair/dt = Hout(Tout − Tair) + Hvent(Tsupply − Tair) + Ham(Tmass − Tair) + Qto air
Cmass · dTmass/dt = Hwall(Tout − Tmass) + Ham(Tair − Tmass) + Qto mass

Heat sources are the radiator, people, lighting and equipment, and the sun through the window. All room numbers are assumed (see the table below); the wall U-value, infiltration and heat split are calibration knobs, not measurements.

Validation benchmarks

Published Swedish school energy-use data, for orders of magnitude only (the replayed buildings are in Oslo).
